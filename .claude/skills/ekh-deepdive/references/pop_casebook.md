# Casebook — how คุณ Pop reads hospitals other than VIH

`analyst_playbook.md` is the core lens and was learned mostly from VIH. This file holds what his posts on other
hospitals add: BCH, CHG, RJH, LPH, THG, NKT, WPH (SSO and provincial), EKH, PR9, RPH, BH, BDMS (cash, insurance,
foreign patients), and the transferable parts of KLINIQ, MASTER, TRP (clinic and surgery chains). §10 is the shape of his
long-form deep dive (ThaiVI presentation on VIH). **Read the section
that matches the stock's situation**, not the whole file. Evidence is given as `TICKER/file` under
`<Thstock>/<TICKER>/md/analyst_notes/fb/`. Posts tagged `shared` are other people's text; rules below come from his
own posts unless marked.

## 0. What to buy, in one filter

Three conditions together: a good business, a new investment, and that investment about to pass break-even. He
rejects a good company long past break-even ("หุ้นดีที่เกินจุด Break even มาไกลผมก็ไม่เอา") and a good company
with no new investment. The reason is the arithmetic of new revenue: with variable cost (drugs, doctor fees) near
40%, 100 baht of extra revenue is 60 of gross profit and 48 after 20% tax — an incremental net margin of ~48% against
an average of ~15%, so revenue +10% gives profit +20–30%. (RPH 2019: `RPH/2019-11-05_p09`, All_Post #3612, #3871.)

Also: **a thesis everyone shares is not an edge.** He sold EKH (waiting for China's reopening for IVF) because every
investor saw the same thing; low expectation matters more than a correct story (`EKH/2022-12-30_p15`). Edge is a
company "ที่เราเองเข้าใจ และ ตลาดยังไม่เข้าใจ".

## 1. SSO-heavy hospitals (BCH, CHG, RJH, LPH, NKT)

- **Unit economics.** SSO revenue ÷ registered members, by year and by hospital. Member growth stops when the main
  hospital's quota is full (RJH: growth then goes through the Rojana branch). Revenue per member rising after that
  means more complex care done in-house and fewer referrals out (`RJH/2020-10-13_p05`). Who the members work for
  matters: factory workers hold up, tourism workers do not.
- **RW>2 is a recurring item, not a 2567 one-off.** Under-payment already existed in 2562–2563. Each hospital chooses
  the rate it accrues (RJH booked 10,679 per point when 12,000 was paid; BCH booked 7,200 in 2567). A conservative
  accrual is hidden upside that lands in one quarter with no cost attached, and it is disclosed only in the MD&A.
  Size it both ways, after 20% tax. Compare the accrual policy across SSO hospitals before comparing their growth.
- **SSO income has parts with different payment risk:** capitation, the 26 chronic diseases, RW>2, dental (BCH meeting
  notes, shared).
- **SSO is a scale-and-cost business.** It works next to a crowded state hospital with few private rivals (RJH); it
  is hard where private SSO hospitals already hold the members, and for an operator whose skill is cash patients
  (NKT's SSO expansion: "กลุ่มรายได้ที่มีความเสี่ยงมาก แต่ กำไรน้อย", `NKT/2024-12-18_p01`). So an SSO base "spreads
  risk" only with scale and cost discipline.
- **Margin by payor.** He estimates SSO margin near 20% and cash plus foreign near 32–34% (his own estimate, BCH):
  when the cash/foreign segment slows, profit falls more than revenue. The net-margin ceiling therefore depends on
  payor mix, not only occupancy.
- **Government receivables.** Watch ageing buckets migrate and size the amount against profit; a government debtor is
  "ที่ไม่น่าจะเบี้ยว ก็ไม่แน่" — SSO budgets have run short before. A peer collecting the same kind of claim is
  evidence the money will come (CHG's accrued COVID income falling eased the worry on BCH).

## 2. Foreign-patient and high-end cash hospitals (PR9, BH, BDMS, EKH's IVF)

- **Its own calendar.** Q4 is weak (Christmas, New Year) and the Ramadan quarter is weak and moves each year. The
  Q3-respiratory pattern of Thai-patient hospitals does not apply.
- **Set a test the thesis must pass.** The clean quarter (no holiday, no Ramadan) must make a new high; "ถ้าทำไม่ได้
  อันนี้อาจจะต้องกลับมาพิจารณา" (`PR9/2026-05-13_p11`).
- **Cross-check with the segment leader.** BH's foreign revenue at a record while PR9's fell means the cause is
  company-specific. BH is also his gauge of high-end Thai demand.
- **Size the market by share of the referrer** (Qatar sends ~10% of its patients to PR9, so the reachable market may
  be ~9× current), supported by the price gap and his own bills as a customer (`PR9/2026-01-19_p15`).
- **Selling expense here is semi-variable and front-loaded:** agent commission plus marketing that comes before the
  revenue. Check management's promise that it will fall against the next three quarters (`PR9/2026-08-13_p07`).
- **The payor split shows case mix.** Insurance revenue at a record while insurers refuse simple-disease claims
  means complex cases, which he calls a moat. Payor lines and nationality lines overlap; never add them.
- **Capacity.** Ask how much is left: BH's demand was "โดนแคปเอาไว้ด้วย Capacity"; PR9 has 204 beds open and can
  open up to 300 in a building whose depreciation is already booked — free operating leverage.
- **A premium hospital does not cut cost in a crisis** (it would hurt confidence), so record revenue after reopening
  does not bring record margin.
- **Foreign specialty units (IVF):** check the channel (one exclusive agent vs several), licences, and rules against
  the treatment cycle (visa on arrival 14 → 30 days vs a 2–3 week IVF cycle) (`EKH/2022-08-24_p26`).
- **COPAY:** for this group he judged it "แทบจะไม่กระทบกับ รพ. เอกชน เลย" and the price fall an overreaction.

## 3. A new hospital or building (RPH, PR9, EKH, BCH, WPH, NKT)

- **Break-even in physical units.** Ask IR for break-even in rooms or beds per day and compare actual use ("break
  even คือ 2 ห้องต่อวัน … ใช้ห้องตึกใหม่ 10 ห้อง/วัน"). A shared method: monthly depreciation ÷ revenue per room.
- **Runway.** Keep your own record of occupied beds vs capacity, beds still to open, and local market share (RPH:
  114 beds ≈ 57% of capacity; share 40% → 60%). It shows how long growth can last.
- **The low-season quarter is the stress test.** For a hospital just past break-even, Q2 is the quarter most likely
  to show profit falling; holding profit there confirms the thesis (`All_Post #3936`).
- **Costs can arrive late.** A building opened in a crisis hides its cost step: PR9's SG&A stayed flat after the 2563
  opening because of COVID cuts, then stepped up ~25 mn a quarter; BCH's three COVID-era hospitals opened with minimal
  equipment and the costs came afterwards. Flat capex and depreciation do not prove the investment phase is over.
- **His test of a successful expansion:** "รายได้นั้นเพิ่มขึ้นมาทันค่าเสื่อมราคา และ SG&A" — and few hospitals pass.
  Separate durable revenue (new departments) from temporary revenue (deferred treatment).
- **Pre-opening staff sits in admin** for about three months, then moves to cost of sales on opening, so SG&A/sales
  cannot fall while openings continue (KLINIQ; the same for a hospital hiring ahead of opening).
- **Depreciation policy** (whole building at opening vs floor by floor) sets the early drag.
- **Test an expansion against the existing trend.** Adding beds while IPD revenue is falling turns the extension into
  pure cost: "ถ้าขยายกิจการแล้วไม่ดี โอกาสก็จะกลายเป็นความเสี่ยง" (NKT).
- **Signs of a sensible build:** open fewer beds first, owned land, big retailers already in the area standing in for
  location research.
- **Strip the new unit's loss** to see base profit, size that loss against group profit, and compute the revenue the
  unit needs to break even. At break-even the loss is added back: that is next period's profit step.
- **Where to look in a network:** bunched openings make profit bottom when the last one opens, then ramp from a low
  base. A profit fall caused only by new-branch costs, with old branches intact, is a different thing from a fall in
  the old branches (`BCH/2026-06-07_p01`). Say which it is; use `LOCATE` and the notes.
- **Roll-out chains (KLINIQ):** size each opening wave as % of the existing base (a 10–18% wave predicts a margin
  dip); read margin by opening-year cohort; the turn from investment to harvest is management choosing to slow
  openings — check the promise against the record. Here the investment phase is opex-led (rent, staff, doctor
  guarantees, launch marketing), so the capex test misses it.
- **Acquisitions are an investment phase too:** cost at once, synergy later (MASTER).

## 4. A windfall and its unwinding (COVID 2564–2565: BCH, CHG, LPH, THG, RPH, VIH)

- **Normal = the pre-shock band.** Use the pre-shock quarterly range for revenue and gross margin (BCH GPM 44% vs a
  normal 30–35%; CHG revenue vs a 1,300–1,500 mn band). List what the excess is made of (pent-up demand, COVID,
  season, new hospitals) and which parts will go.
- **QoQ is the right view here** — YoY means nothing while the base is a windfall.
- **Leverage in reverse.** Line up the revenue change against the cost change quarter by quarter. Costs that fall more
  slowly than revenue are either a lag (harmless) or a new cost base; admin is hard to cut back.
- **After a reopening record, four questions (BH):** is revenue only back to the old level, is it above normal
  (pent-up), is more still to come, is there capacity?
- **Timing.** He does not buy while revenue is still above normal, even if the price has fallen: "mode ของการติดตาม
  เพื่อหาจังหวะที่เหมาะสม". The report states this as a fact about the base, not as advice.
- **First reporter previews the group.** Read the first hospital's results as a preview of the others.

## 5. Checks on the statements (any hospital)

- **Match an asset line to a liability line.** Prepaid vaccine in other receivables ÷ cost per dose = doses bought;
  customer deposits ÷ selling price = doses sold; the gap is unsold stock, best and worst case. Hospitals normally
  have no deposits ("รักษาแล้วค่อยเก็บเงิน"), so an unusual liability is a clue. Passing the check does not make the
  stock cheap (THG had sold out and was still expensive).
- **Compare the 6-month and 9-month cash-flow statements** to find one-offs the P&L does not show (inventory
  write-down, fair-value loss), when margin looks wrong for the revenue level (`CHG/2022-11-21_p03`). The detail of
  other income (a dividend received) is also in the cash-flow statement.
- **Compare at the same revenue level.** Gross margin and each cost as a band at a given revenue range, and as % of
  revenue against pre-shock years. Equal revenue with different gross profit means one quarter's cost was abnormal.
- **Fixed or variable?** Watch a cost in the seasonally weak quarter: if it does not fall when revenue does, it is
  fixed. The variable-cost ratio can be read from QoQ changes (cost +6.5 on revenue +11 ≈ 59%).
- **Segment P&L from the two sets of statements:** separate = one business, subsidiaries = another (EKH: hospital vs
  IVF). Give each entity its own phase.
- **Rebuild a normal base from a truncated period** (one month of pre-COVID IVF revenue × 3).
- **Receivables ageing** rising for three quarters with the allowance unchanged is a forward warning. He infers the
  debtor, then confirms with IR and says which it is.
- **Small reads:** finance cost near zero against large liabilities = non-interest-bearing debt; finance cost falling
  for several quarters can allow a higher payout; depreciation rising faster than the number of units = equipment
  deepening, not footprint; a shock on the last weekend of a quarter moves revenue across the boundary and also
  creates next year's base.
- **After-tax sizing.** He compares growth at profit before tax, but sizes a one-off or an incremental margin after a
  flat 20% tax.

## 6. Demand drivers and seasonality

- **The mechanism is the school calendar.** Term opens in mid-Q2 and infections build into Q3; a late opening, masks
  and temperature checks remove that revenue. Adults with chronic disease are stable; children are volatile.
- **A missing epidemic is late, not gone.** He rejects "vaccination removed the epidemic" as an explanation for one
  quarter, since vaccines have existed for years; he checks occupancy at several hospitals instead (2026-09-06).
- **Volume up: market or share?** If a disease were really up 30%, the ministry would be warning the public. If not,
  patients are moving from other hospitals (price, brand).
- **Low-margin services recruit patients** (check-ups, vaccines).
- **"Price war" is tested against gross margin.** If GPM is not falling, the price cutting is advertising language.

## 7. Valuation, results day, and when the thesis fails

- **A high P/E needs a visible, near driver.** He held RPH at 35× normal profit only because an idle old hospital
  could reopen; when management shelved it, "PE ที่สูงอาจจะใช้เวลาที่นานกว่าเดิม กว่าที่กำไรจะเติบโต"
  (`RPH/2022-08-10_p02`). A single-site hospital with a local near-monopoly can carry a high P/E only with such an
  option in sight.
- **Trailing P/E falls mechanically** when prior-year quarters with special expenses roll out. Say which quarters.
- **PEG = 1 as fair** in his own voice (growth 20–40% ↔ P/E 20–40×); forward P/E just after capacity opens as the
  valuation floor. The report shows the numbers and never the verdict.
- **Cheap P/E with high growth is not enough** in a cyclical: P/E is lowest at peak margin; decide whether the change
  is temporary or permanent (authorship of this post uncertain).
- **The record chain:** Q2 at a record → Q3 at a record → record cash flow → excess cash → higher payout. The
  dividend then has two engines, profit and payout.
- **Peer line-up by expansion type** (`BCH/2026-06-07_p01`): branch count (50+ gains little from one more), capacity
  added as % of base, same-site vs new-site, spare beds in a depreciated building, pricing power vs capacity limit,
  whether star doctors follow to a new site.
- **Diagnose a miss by its reference point.** A miss against consensus caused by other or finance income is "in
  line". A miss against management's own target leaves marketing sized for revenue that did not come, which can be
  cut at once. SG&A is easier to control than gross margin ("เป็นการบริหารจัดการภายใน").
- **Results in line and a falling price is not a reason to sell**; he watches dividend growth, holds 3–4 years or as
  long as growth continues: "ดีไม่เท่าที่เราคิด แต่ตราบเท่าที่มันยังดีและเติบโต".
- **Holding through a falling price:** revenue, profit and dividend must keep making new highs, and he re-reads his
  written thesis for errors.
- **When he gives up:** cost lines he cannot explain with no IR to ask; an expected recovery that does not show while
  a cheaper stock is available; a growth driver shelved; a thesis everyone already holds. A correct cost thesis still
  fails if revenue does not come.
- **IPO checks:** unknown low-cost pre-IPO holders, the share free of lock-up, IPO P/E against listed peers (NKT).
- **Management signals:** guidance that has not been overstated, insiders holding a large stake, an IPO that sold no
  existing shares, learning from a past shock, real money into a new unit.

## 8. Mistakes he admits

- A margin gap is a hypothesis until IR confirms the cause (he read SABINA's GPM drop as inflation; it was channel
  mix).
- He recorded a wrong forecast of the bottom quarter openly (RPH).
- All-in with margin on one stock nearly ended him; after it, no all-in and no margin.
- Selling a stock too early because of price, not thesis (MASTER).

## 9. Reading the posts

- Tags are a heuristic. Broker notes (KKP, KS, DAOL), meeting notes ("By สมาคมนักลงทุน…", IAT), Stock Vitamins
  ("วิตามินหุ้น"), MrStock summaries and press releases are not his analysis even when the tag says `own`.

  its inputs). Every figure is checked against the pack before use.

## 10. The long-form deep dive (his ThaiVI presentation on VIH, price as of 16 June 2026)

Source: `VIH/md/second_source/VIH_Deepdive.txt` (tier 2; cite as `second_source/VIH_Deepdive:L<n>`). This is the
only place he lays out a whole case in order. Use it for the *shape* of a deep-dive report; every figure in it is
checked against the filings before use (his spoken numbers slip — see `conflicts` in the VIH narrative).

**Order of the argument**
1. Industry demand and supply first (ageing, chronic disease; beds and doctors).
2. Name the threat people fear, then **test it against history**. Public hospitals kept adding capacity for ten years;
   listed private hospitals' revenue still grew. A threat that has been present throughout the record and did not stop
   growth is not a new risk. Say what would make it different this time.
3. The company's branches: share of revenue and profit by branch, and **which branches own land they can build on**.
4. What changed recently (second-generation management, contracts dropped, new centres).
5. Quality of care and the doctor pipeline — the thing a new building cannot buy.
6. Location and the local competitors, hospital by hospital. A full competitor that cannot expand sends patients over.
7. The record: revenue, profit and DPS over 3 and 10 years. A dividend case needs all three rising together.
8. The next S-curve (new hospital): the IFA's projection, sized against a listed analogue's actual ramp-up.
9. Why this stock: a peer table of P/E, occupancy, yield and payout.
10. Risks. For a company building a new hospital, the biggest risk and the biggest opportunity are the same thing.

**Occupancy is distance from two points, not a score**
- Around 50–55% the hospital is not far past break-even: fixed cost is covered, so added revenue falls mostly to
  profit. This is where operating leverage is strongest (revenue +5%, normal profit +19%).
- Around 80% the hospital is full. Without land to add beds, growth stops there — "เห็น utilization 80 ก็ต้องขาย".
  With land beside the hospital, 80% becomes the trigger for the next building. So always pair occupancy with
  expansion room.
- Occupancy is rarely in the quarterly filings. Take it from the One Report or Oppday; otherwise it is a gap.

**Think in baht, not percent**
- At 4–5% industry growth the percent looks dull. Ask instead: how many baht of revenue were added, and how many
  reached net profit. His rule of thumb: incremental gross margin about 50%, fixed cost flat, tax 20% → about 40
  satang of each added baht.
- Compute it from the statements (ΔNP / Δrevenue), and strip base-year one-offs first. Another speaker's objection is
  valid and must be shown: part of the profit gain comes from efficiency on *existing* revenue, not only from the
  added revenue, so the ratio overstates what the next baht will bring.

**New hospital vs extension**
- A new building beside an existing hospital shares equipment, doctors and nurses' overtime; it costs far less to run
  than a stand-alone hospital and loses less in year one.
- Opening a new hospital *later* is safer: the larger the existing profit base, the smaller the first-year loss
  (about 100 mn in his VIH sizing) is against it.
- Size the opportunity by an existing analogue's share of its owner's profit. RJH's branch in the neighbouring
  province is a small share of RJH's profit, so a strong local name does not guarantee a large prize.

**Volume can fall for good reasons**
- Dropping check-up contracts with ~1% margin lowers visits and frees capacity for better work.
- Falling inpatient days can mean faster bed turnover or cases performed at partner hospitals. Check revenue per
  case and margin before calling it weakness.

**The counter-view he was given (keep it in the risk section)**
Industry growth has slowed to 4–5%: patients trade down, companies cut health packages, insurers push back on claims,
capacity built after COVID is arriving, and at flat volume fixed cost works in reverse. A deep dive states this
view and says which of these the company's own numbers already show.
